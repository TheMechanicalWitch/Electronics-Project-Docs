\subsection{Nonlinearisation of the potentiometer}

The behaviour between the potentiometer derived angle and the measured external angle was non linear. 
Several different solutions were tried amongst them: changing the "ContinuousServoConfig" variable in robot\_shoulder\_module.c by trail and error \&
writing non linear version of "LIMB\_LERP\_FROM\_RANGE" function in limb\_utils.h. Neither of these gave satesfactory results.

A remaining avenue is the behaviour of the potentiometer themselves. While unmounted the potentiometer stops while at its max \& min values. though while mounted the arm is able to rotate it more than its designated 285 degrees. 

\subsection{Arm position from Vision}
The vision system is a real-time human tracking system that uses an OAK-D camera together with MediaPipe and OpenCV. The camera provides both an RGB video stream and depth information, which allows the system to detect not only where a person's body and hands are in the image, but also their position in 3D space. MediaPipe is used to detect the person's pose and identify important landmarks such as the shoulders, elbows, and wrists. The depth information is then used to calculate the X, Y, and Z coordinates of these points in meters. 

For each detected body joint, the program takes its 2D pixel position (u, v) and combines it with the depth value Z from the stereo camera. Using the camera's focal lengths and optical center, it calculates the 3D position with the formulas X = (u - cx)Z / fx and Y = (v - cy)Z / fy. This gives the position of the shoulder, elbow, and wrist in meters relative to the camera.  The code also uses vector mathematics to calculate joint angles. 

\subsection{Hand position from Vision}
For the arm, it creates two vectors from the elbow to the shoulder and from the elbow to the wrist. The angle between these vectors is calculated using the dot product formula \begin{math}
    \cos(\theta) = \frac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|}%\cos(\Theta) = (a · b) / (|a||b|)
\end{math}. The same principle is used for the fingers to determine whether they are open or closed. If the calculated angle is below a certain threshold, the finger is considered closed, otherwise it is considered open.  

For hand orientation, the code uses the cross product of two vectors formed between the wrist, index finger, and pinky. The resulting vector represents the normal direction of the palm. By looking at the Z-component of this normal vector, the program estimates whether the palm is facing towards or away from the camera.


\section{Theory}

\subsection{Invalability of extracting angles from the IMU}

Any angle measurment that can be extracted from the IMU will be realative to its own frame of reference, it only measures decomposition
of the gravitational vector on its own frame of reference\footnote{
    The datasheet states 6 degrees of freedom which is a half truth, it can not measure lateral movements. it gives a gyro
    reading that is achieved through onboard integration of the instantaneous change of the accelerometer (decomposed gravity) readings.
}. For the LIMB this caused an issue for two reasons.
First the IMU frame of reference cannot be constrained to be orthogonally paralell to the frame of reference assigned to the LIMB. Secondly the LIMB lacks inherent
azimuth motion. Since the IMU can only reliable measure rotation in one of its own planes, this breaks in the LIMB since it couldnt be placed in the bicep with one 
of its axis paralell to the gravitational vector. Because of this any elevation of the arm would have to be calculated by full 3-dimension translation, which is mathematically
impossible with only an IMU. The information density is insufficient. Secondly standard assumptions when calculating azimuth and elevation is an elevation angle out from the gravitational vector
and a rotation around the gravitational vector. This does not hold for the LIMB however, since both azimuth and elevation of the arm is caused
by the two shoulder joints. they both angle out from the gravitational vector but orthogonal to eachother. This means that the IMU cant tell if a roation is caused by an up-down or a left-right movement,
nor can it tell if a change is an azimuth change caused by dual shoulder joint; or an elivation change caused by a single shoulder joint movement.
